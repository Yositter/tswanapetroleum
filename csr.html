Corporate social responsibility

Fuel is a responsibility, not only a product

We move dangerous goods across borders, through communities and onto working sites every day. How we do that shapes lives, livelihoods and the environment along seven corridors. Our responsibility programme is built into operations, not bolted onto them.

Five pillars

Each pillar carries commitments we can measure and report against. They map to the way South African buyers, financiers and regulators already assess a supplier.

Responsibility in fuel distribution is concrete. A sealed compartment prevents theft and adulteration. A trained driver prevents a crash. A local transporter developed into a viable business keeps money in the region. A clear code of conduct prevents the quiet corrosion of bribery. We treat each of these as an operational control with an owner, a target and a record.

Our framework aligns to the Broad-Based Black Economic Empowerment codes, to POPIA and PAIA for information governance, and to recognised sustainability reporting standards so that our disclosures stand up to the scrutiny of financiers and listed customers.

Commitments we hold ourselves to

  1. Safety of people first, every load, every day

    Every compartment is sealed at the gantry and logged, every truck is tracked by GPS from the control room, and every delivery is metered with the customer's team present. Our target is zero harm to people and zero uncontrolled product loss.

  2. Protect the environment we operate in

    We plan for spill prevention and response at every site, supply low-sulphur 10 ppm diesel for cleaner combustion, and measure and reduce the carbon intensity of our own fleet and facilities year on year.

  3. Develop the people we employ

    We invest in accredited driver training, dangerous-goods handling, and learnerships that create a path from entry roles into skilled and supervisory work, with a focus on youth and women in a male-dominated industry.

  4. Grow local enterprise along our corridors

    We prefer local suppliers and transporters, support owner-drivers to build viable businesses, and structure supplier development in line with the B-BBEE codes rather than as a paperwork exercise.

  5. Trade honestly and transparently

    Our employee and supplier codes of conduct prohibit bribery, fraud and unfair dealing. We honour POPIA and PAIA, collect the minimum personal information needed, and make our information manual available on request.

  6. Support the communities that host us

    We direct social investment toward road safety education, energy access and skills near our hubs and along the corridors we serve, working with local partners rather than imposing from outside.

Governance holds it together

Commitments without conduct rules are slogans. Ours are written down and binding.

Two codes of conduct set the standard for behaviour. The employee code governs how our people work, drive, handle product and treat one another. The supplier code sets the terms we expect of every transporter, vendor and partner who carries the Tswana Petroleum name.
